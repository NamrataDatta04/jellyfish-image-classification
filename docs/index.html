Bag or Background?

Sea turtles mistake plastic bags for jellyfish. This classifier tries not to. Upload an underwater photo and compare a normally fine-tuned network with the same network after a shortcut fix (last-layer retraining, DFR). Your image never leaves your device.

The model only knows two classes. Any other image (a fish, a person, a cat) is still forced into “plastic bag” or “jellyfish”.

What to try

  • Click “Empty deep-sea background”. There is nothing in the picture, but the training bags were all filmed by deep-sea robots, so the dark, murky water alone pushes the model towards “plastic bag”.
  • Click “Empty clear-water background”. Clear blue water looks like the jellyfish photos, so it pulls towards “jellyfish”.
  • Upload a plastic bag in clear or bright water. This is the case the standard model gets wrong most often; the DFR version is right far more often.

The fix helps but does not remove the shortcut: both versions still give confident answers for empty backgrounds, which is why the project also studies rejecting images with no object.

This is the source shortcut the project measures: in every public dataset we could combine, bags and jellyfish come from different cameras and different water, so a network can score highly by recognising the background instead of the object.

How this model scores on the background benchmark

Accuracy on held-out test images where the object and the background are mixed and matched (seed 813). “Worst group” is the lowest of the first four rows. About

Part of Bag or Background? Diagnosing and Mitigating Source Shortcuts in Underwater Plastic-Bag vs. Jellyfish Classification by Namrata Datta, Neesha Naimeen and Banupriya Thamilvanan (Auckland University of Technology, 2026). Code, results and method: GitHub repository.

The network runs on your device with ONNX Runtime Web. A research demo only: it is not suitable for real monitoring, and the model weights are for non-commercial research use (see the repository licence).
